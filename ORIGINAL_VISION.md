# MARA — preserved source vision

This file preserves the creative contract that the project is being built against. `GAME_DESIGN.md` is the canonical production design; this is the source brief and its non-negotiable intent.

## The promise

Build a large, original 2D indie dating/life simulator that begins as a genuinely good, warm school-and-town game and eventually becomes extremely dark, intimate, surreal, cruel and emotionally exhausting. It must retain beautiful, funny and romantic moments. The horror cannot be an occasional genre garnish, a tidy murder mystery, or a final boss that explains and solves everything.

The player should live in Larkspur long enough to love its music, routes, bedrooms, shops, jokes, dates and people. Horror gains power by violating things the player remembers being safe. Nothing is frightening unless the player remembers when it was not.

## Mara

Her entire known name is **Mara**. Never give her a surname. Do not establish a reliable age, address, family, course, origin or biography. All romanceable characters are adults aged 18 or older.

Mara is the central constant: the romance option, stalker, murderer, comforter, thing outside the window, crying girl, furious girl and calm girl sitting beside Alex the next morning. She genuinely loves the player and she is genuinely evil. She is not misunderstood, secretly good, redeemable or fixable. The protagonist never becomes her. She cannot be permanently killed, and the story never becomes a conventional weapon quest or boss battle.

Mara must be volatile and recognizably human. She can be sweet, silly, shy, clingy, flirtatious, protective, embarrassed and childishly delighted. She can also be jealous, manipulative, cruel, panicked, pathetic, furious and inconsolable. She may scream, beg, send embarrassing messages, destroy something and then stop mid-sob, wipe her face, smile and quietly ask whether Alex wants company walking home. Her stillness should eventually be more frightening than her rage.

She notices routes, seats, calls, messages, sleep, gifts, lies, attraction and time spent in other people's homes. Sometimes she knows things no observation channel can explain. She is not perfectly omniscient: Alex can successfully lie, hide, investigate, protect someone temporarily or follow her. The player should often be unsure whether a secret succeeded.

Most Mara dialogue is normal. Rare impossible remarks—“Your room looked nicer the first time,” “You cried more last time,” “I hate Thursdays,” “Don't take the train tomorrow”—are scarce enough to be screenshotted and debated. She never supplies helpful lore afterward.

## What she is

The internal creative truth is that Mara is demonic. The game never says this outright and never provides a taxonomy or a “demon reveal” chapter. Her nature appears through contradictions: wrong shadows, delayed reflections, one bad animation frame, impossible travel, old photographs, predictive slips, animal fear, temperature, light, survival and music.

Very brief horn- or tail-like shapes are allowed on exceptionally cruel or impossible lines, but only as scarce frame-length punctuation. They cannot become a routine transformation, explicit explanation or monster form. More common tells should be smaller: the leaf clip changes side, eyes face the player, a reflection blinks late, her sprite is one pixel taller, or a smile outlives the dialogue.

## Ordinary life

The opening hours must work without horror. Players explore, attend or skip lessons, choose seats, join clubs, play mini-games, take small jobs, shop, decorate their room, collect items, study, text, call, date, visit friends, attend events, walk home, spend evenings alone and discover side quests. The school, house and town require many memorable authored locations.

NPCs keep schedules and relationships with one another. They argue, date, break up, reconcile, spread rumours, cancel plans, struggle at home and disappear without waiting for the player. Other characters must be strong enough to become favourites before Mara turns affection into danger.

The player's bedroom begins as the safest, warmest place in the game. Its music, lighting, computer, phone, photos and collected objects make returning home pleasurable. Intrusions begin almost beneath notice: a drawer, curtain, photograph or pillow changes; the music gains three familiar notes; the window is unlocked; a text describes the room.

## Horror and investigation

Deaths and disappearances are varied, consequential and personal. A victim may be dead, missing, erased from records, said to have moved, returned altered, or still sending messages. Their empty chair, unfinished quest, vanished jokes and damaged relationships remain. Sometimes the player knows Mara did it; sometimes evidence refuses to agree.

Investigation is playable and dangerous: records, newspapers, photographs, locked areas, belongings, phone metadata, surveillance and following Mara at night. Discovery should produce leverage and contradictions, never one exposition document. Being caught need not kill Alex; it may be worse if Mara simply knows that Alex knows and behaves normally afterward.

The game itself may lie. Dialogue choices, diary entries, messages, photographs, dates, rooms and memories can change. The story does not resolve whether this is Mara, manipulation, supernatural revision or the protagonist's mind.

Rare events are conditional and probabilistic: a camera figure, a voice on the PA, a text from Alex's number, a missing NPC day, Mara on a passing train, a changed photograph, everyone watching an empty corner, a bedroom light already on. Runs should differ. Progression-critical information cannot depend on a rare roll.

One authored Thursday event makes the college obey the wrong weather and wrong hour, breaks the image into displaced strips, and exposes a single frame that players may not be sure they saw. It is an original use of sudden contrast and timing, not a recreation of another game's scene.

## Music

Music is a principal game system. Morning, home, school, town, friends, romance, rain, night, loneliness, dates, characters, locations and story beats need strong melodies. Mara's theme begins beautiful and sincerely romantic. Its six-note contour quietly infects unrelated tracks until the soundtrack feels occupied.

Warm, jealous, sad, possessive, angry, stalking, nightmare and late-game Mara arrangements must be recomposed variants rather than static-plus-noise effects. Stems can crossfade, disappear, swap instrumentation and preserve melody through scenes where players expect silence. Cheerful school music may continue while a friend's seat remains empty. Silence uses footsteps, clothes, rain, fluorescent hum, trains, breathing, doors and phone vibration as composition.

Eventually three or four notes should frighten the player. The game never explains the infection.

## Art and animation

Use a cohesive handcrafted, low-native-resolution 2D look with clearly clustered pixels, limited palettes and nearest-neighbour scaling. The target is the warmth and clarity of beloved small indie RPGs without copying any one game. Generated masters are raw material, never the runtime look: crop, clean, downsample, palette-limit and inspect every sheet.

Mara follows the supplied authority: adult, long red-brown wavy half-up hair with crown loop and burgundy ribbon, brass leaf clip, amber-brown eyes, moss cardigan, cream lace blouse and plaid skirt. She must naturally belong in a cute romance game.

Environments must be authored places that players can memorize, with deliberate normal and altered versions. Avoid building the visible world primarily from flat rectangles or indiscriminately stretched atlases. Horror uses sprite timing, positioning, lighting, camera, portraits, silence and tiny visual inconsistencies rather than demanding elaborate animation.

Characters need individual typewriter rhythm and voice bleeps, readable walk cycles, expression libraries and everyday/distress poses. Mara needs dozens of normal, romantic, wounded, jealous, furious, still and subtly wrong states—not six generic faces.

## Endings

Major endings require distinct final chapters and different kinds of cost. Targets include Perfect Life, Run, The Last Person, Don't Look Behind You, Agreement, The Wrong Person, Open the Door, Normal Ending, She Cries, The Phone and Empty Save, plus further original outcomes. An apparent happy ending may be false; an agreement may genuinely protect others while sacrificing Alex; escape may last months before “found you :)”. No ending permanently kills Mara or turns Alex into her.

## Production standard

Use modular, data-driven systems for maps, schedules, dialogue, relationships, jealousy, fear, trust, suspicion, time, weather, inventory, quests, phone, dates, music, stalking, random events, investigation, save/load, endings and world corruption. Preserve all source assets, prompts, design documents and runtime derivatives in Git.

Do not stop at planning. Maintain a substantial playable opening, test it directly, inspect the rendered game and its code, keep reproducible QA entry points, capture screenshots, and treat visual defects or broken transitions as failures to fix rather than work for the player to diagnose.
